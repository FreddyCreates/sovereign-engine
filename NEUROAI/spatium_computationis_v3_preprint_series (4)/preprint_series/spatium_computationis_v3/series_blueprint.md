## Series Thesis

Spatium Computationis v3 proposes that autonomous intelligence systems can be modeled as agents embedded in a physically constrained computational substrate, where simulation dynamics, differential geometry, cosmological sandboxing, and spatial agent routing become separable but connected layers of system design.

## Recommended Series Structure

### Paper 1: Numerical Mechanics Substrate

Title: **A Symplectic Physics Substrate for Autonomous Agent Simulation**

Distinct thesis: A physics substrate using Velocity Verlet integration, N-body interactions, relativistic speed bounds, elastic collisions, and conservation diagnostics can provide a bounded numerical environment for modeling agents as physical entities.

Primary contribution: numerical mechanics and validation framework.

Do not cover in depth: curvature tensors, mini-verse cosmology, agent battleground taxonomy.

### Paper 2: Riemannian Geometry Engine

Title: **Numerical Riemannian Geometry for Runtime-Modifiable Computational Spacetimes**

Distinct thesis: A metric-function-first geometry engine can compute Christoffel symbols, geodesics, curvature tensors, horizons, and parallel transport numerically, enabling runtime-modifiable spacetime models without requiring closed-form symbolic derivations.

Primary contribution: computational differential geometry engine.

Do not cover in depth: N-body force laws, cosmological topology catalog, threat-routing architecture.

### Paper 3: Mini-Verse Cosmology and Topological Sandboxes

Title: **Mini-Verse Sandboxes: Configurable Cosmological Substrates for Intelligence Experiments**

Distinct thesis: Isolated mini-verses with configurable constants, topologies, Friedmann evolution, entropy accounting, and bounded inter-verse channels can serve as experimental substrates for studying agent behavior under varied physical laws.

Primary contribution: universe-level sandbox architecture.

Do not cover in depth: integrator derivations, full curvature implementation, 21-agent spatial taxonomy.

### Paper 4: Agent Embedding and Battleground Architecture

Title: **Embedding Autonomous Agents in Simulated Spacetime: A Battleground Architecture for Routing, Isolation, and Coordination**

Distinct thesis: Spatially embedding agents and zones inside a simulated substrate allows routing, quarantine, coordination, and influence to be expressed as geometric and physical relationships rather than only abstract software calls.

Primary contribution: autonomous agent architecture and spatial operational model.

Do not cover in depth: full numerical physics validation, tensor derivations, cosmological Friedmann analysis.

## Publication Order

1. Paper 1 establishes the numerical substrate and validation norms.
2. Paper 2 extends the substrate into geometric computation.
3. Paper 3 packages geometry and physics into isolated universe-like experimental domains.
4. Paper 4 applies the substrate to autonomous intelligence architecture.

## Shared Lineage Rule

## Public/Private Boundary

Public-safe:

- Mathematical definitions of metrics, geodesics, conservation laws, and cosmological equations.
- General claim that the architecture proposes physically grounded agent simulation.
- High-level mini-verse taxonomy and sandbox framing.

Needs evidence before public promotion:

- Production-grade implementation claims.
- Quantitative validation thresholds such as energy error below `10^-6`.
- Performance claims such as suitability for `N < 10000`.

IP/release review first:

- Detailed battleground routing logic.
- Security-oriented tiering and adversary-lab semantics.
- Exact agent placements if treated as strategic operational architecture.
